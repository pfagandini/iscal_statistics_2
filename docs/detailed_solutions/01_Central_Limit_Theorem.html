Question 1

The number of accesses per day: \(X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda = 30)\).

1(a)

Exact probability that, in a given day, the number of accesses is higher than 40 is 0.0323. The approximate probability using CLT differs from the exact result by 0.0049. (Marked TRUE)

Step 1: Exact probability

We want
\[ P(X > 40) = 1 - P(X \le 40) = 1 - \sum_{k=0}^{40} e^{-30} \frac{30^k}{k!}. \]

This is computed numerically (using a calculator or software).
If you compute \(P(X \le 40)\) and subtract from 1, you get approximately \(0.0323\).

Step 2: Normal approximation (CLT)

For a Poisson(\(\lambda\)), when \(\lambda\) is large,
\[ X \approx N(\mu = \lambda, \sigma^2 = \lambda). \]

So here: \(\mu = 30\), \(\sigma = \sqrt{30} \approx 5.477\).

We approximate \(P(X > 40)\) by \(P(Y > 40.5)\) with continuity correction, where \(Y \sim N(30, 30)\).

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{40.5 - 30}{\sqrt{30}} \approx \frac{10.5}{5.477} \approx 1.917. \]

Then \[ P(X > 40) \approx P(Z > 1.917) = 1 - \Phi(1.917) \approx 0.0274 \quad (\text{approx}). \]

Step 3: Error of approximation

Exact: about \(0.0323\).
Normal approximation: about \(0.0274\).
Difference in absolute value: \(|0.0323 - 0.0274| \approx 0.0049\).

1(b)

Weekly number of accesses in 7 days. Let \(S_7 = X_1 + \cdots + X_7\).

Each day is Poisson(30) and independent, so
\[ S_7 \sim \text{Poisson}(7 \cdot 30) = \text{Poisson}(210). \]

We want \(P(200 < S_7 \le 220)\) approximately.

Step 1: CLT approximation

For a Poisson with large mean \(\lambda = 210\):
\[ S_7 \approx N(\mu = 210, \sigma^2 = 210). \]

Use continuity correction from 200.5 to 220.5:

\[ P(200 < S_7 \le 220) \approx P(200.5 \le Y \le 220.5), \] where \(Y \sim N(210, 210)\).

Step 2: Standardize bounds

\[ Z_1 = \frac{200.5 - 210}{\sqrt{210}},\quad Z_2 = \frac{220.5 - 210}{\sqrt{210}}. \]

\(\sqrt{210} \approx 14.491\).

Then: \[ Z_1 \approx \frac{-9.5}{14.491} \approx -0.656, \] \[ Z_2 \approx \frac{10.5}{14.491} \approx 0.725. \]

Step 3: Use standard normal CDF

\[ P(200.5 \le Y \le 220.5) = \Phi(0.725) - \Phi(-0.656). \]

But \(\Phi(-a) = 1 - \Phi(a)\). So:

\[ \Phi(0.725) - \Phi(-0.656) = \Phi(0.725) - (1 - \Phi(0.656)) = \Phi(0.725)+\Phi(0.656) - 1. \]

Using a normal table or calculator, \(\Phi(0.725) \approx 0.766\) and \(\Phi(0.656)\approx 0.744\).

So approximate probability: \[ 0.766 + 0.744 - 1 = 0.51 \text{ (around)}. \]

More precise computation gives ~0.5098.

Question 2

System with 100 parts, independent, each fails with probability \(p = 0.1\).
Let \(X\) be the number of failed parts: \(X \sim \text{Binomial}(n=100, p=0.1)\).
Number of working parts is \(100 - X\).

System works if at least 85 parts are operative. That means at most 15 failed:

\[ \text{System works} \iff X \le 15. \]

2(a)

Statement: approximate probability that system works is 0.9664. (Marked TRUE)

So we need \(P(X \le 15)\).

Step 1: Parameters for normal approximation

Mean: \[ \mu = np = 100 \cdot 0.1 = 10. \]

Variance: \[ \sigma^2 = np(1-p) = 100 \cdot 0.1 \cdot 0.9 = 9. \]

\(\sigma = 3\).

Step 2: CLT with continuity correction

We want \(P(X \le 15)\). With continuity correction: \[ P(X \le 15) \approx P(Y \le 15.5), \quad Y \sim N(10, 9). \]

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{15.5 - 10}{3} = \frac{5.5}{3} \approx 1.833. \]

So \[ P(X \le 15) \approx \Phi(1.833). \]

Using a standard normal table, \(\Phi(1.83)\) is around 0.9664.

2(b)

Statement: In the previous calculation, using CLT requires only that sample size from a non-normal population is large enough. (Marked FALSE)

Explanation

Conditions to use CLT approximation for binomial also include that both \(np\) and \(n(1-p)\) are “not too small,” typically \(np \ge 5\) and \(n(1-p) \ge 5\). The statement that only a “large sample” is enough ignores this condition.

Thus the statement is FALSE, marking is correct.

Question 3

TeleDigit calls. Each call is a complaint with probability \(p = 0.05\).

3(a)

Sample of 20 calls. Let \(X \sim \text{Binomial}(n=20, p=0.05)\).
We want \(P(2 \le X \le 5)\).

Statement: that probability equals 0.2639 (marked TRUE).

Step 1: Exact binomial probability

\[ P(2 \le X \le 5) = \sum_{k=2}^5 \binom{20}{k} (0.05)^k (0.95)^{20-k}. \]

Compute term by term:

  • For \(k=2\): \(\binom{20}{2} (0.05)^2 (0.95)^{18}\).
  • For \(k=3\): \(\binom{20}{3} (0.05)^3 (0.95)^{17}\).
  • For \(k=4\): \(\binom{20}{4} (0.05)^4 (0.95)^{16}\).
  • For \(k=5\): \(\binom{20}{5} (0.05)^5 (0.95)^{15}\).

Using a calculator or software, the sum gives approximately 0.2639.

Verdict

The value 0.2639 is correct; statement is TRUE, marking is correct.

3(b)

Sample of 250 calls. Let \(Y \sim \text{Binomial}(n=250, p=0.05)\).
We want approximate \(P(Y \le 10)\).

Step 1: Parameters for CLT

Mean: \[ \mu = np = 250 \cdot 0.05 = 12.5. \]

Variance: \[ \sigma^2 = np(1-p) = 12.5 \cdot 0.95 = 11.875. \]

\(\sigma \approx \sqrt{11.875} \approx 3.447\).

Step 2: Continuity correction

We want \(P(Y \le 10)\). Use \(10.5\):

\[ P(Y \le 10) \approx P(Z \le z_0), \quad z_0 = \frac{10.5 - 12.5}{3.447} \approx \frac{-2}{3.447} \approx -0.58. \]

Step 3: Normal CDF

\[ P(Y \le 10) \approx \Phi(-0.58) = 1 - \Phi(0.58). \]

\(\Phi(0.58) \approx 0.719\), so

\[ P(Y \le 10) \approx 1 - 0.719 = 0.281. \]

Thus approximately 0.2810.

Question 4

Daily orders of machines: \(X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda=9.5)\).

4(a)

We want percentage of days where more than 10 machines are ordered:

\[ P(X > 10) = 1 - P(X \le 10). \]

The options include 35.47% as the marked answer.

Step 1: Exact or approximate computation

Compute \(P(X \le 10) = \sum_{k=0}^{10} e^{-9.5} \frac{9.5^k}{k!}\).
Numerically this yields approximately 0.6453.

Then: \[ P(X > 10) = 1 - 0.6453 = 0.3547 \implies 35.47\%. \]

4(b)

4 work days. Total machines shipped in 4 days:
\(S_4 = X_1 + \cdots + X_4\), each \(X_i \sim \text{Poisson}(9.5)\).

So \(S_4 \sim \text{Poisson}(4 \cdot 9.5) = \text{Poisson}(38)\).

They give that the exact probability of having sent fewer than 30 machines in 4 days is 0.097964. We want to approximate this by CLT and compare.

Step 1: CLT approximation

For a Poisson(38), approximate with \(Y \sim N(\mu=38,\sigma^2=38)\).

We want \(P(S_4 < 30) = P(S_4 \le 29)\).

Use continuity correction:

\[ P(S_4 \le 29) \approx P(Y \le 29.5). \]

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{29.5 - 38}{\sqrt{38}} = \frac{-8.5}{\sqrt{38}}. \]

\(\sqrt{38} \approx 6.164\), so \[ Z \approx \frac{-8.5}{6.164} \approx -1.38. \]

Then:

\[ P(Y \le 29.5) \approx \Phi(-1.38) = 1 - \Phi(1.38). \]

\(\Phi(1.38) \approx 0.9162\), so \[ P(Y \le 29.5) \approx 1 - 0.9162 = 0.0838. \]

Exact probability: 0.07964.
Approximate: about 0.0838–0.07964.
The absolute error is about 0.00416.

Question 5

Projector lifetime: 20% fail in the first year.

So for each projector, define \(X_i = 1\) if it fails in the first year, 0 otherwise, with \(P(X_i=1) = p = 0.2\).

In sample of 500 projectors, number of failures \(Y \sim \text{Binomial}(n=500, p=0.2)\).

We want approximate probability that at most 80 fail.

Step 1: Parameters

Mean: \[ \mu = np = 500 \cdot 0.2 = 100. \]

Variance: \[ \sigma^2 = np(1-p) = 500 \cdot 0.2 \cdot 0.8 = 80. \]

\(\sigma = \sqrt{80} \approx 8.944\).

Step 2: Continuity correction

\(P(Y \le 80)\) is approximated by \[ P(Y \le 80) \approx P(Z \le z_0), \quad z_0 = \frac{80.5 - 100}{8.944}. \]

\[ z_0 \approx \frac{-19.5}{8.944} \approx -2.18. \]

So \[ P(Y \le 80) \approx \Phi(-2.18) = 1 - \Phi(2.18). \]

\(\Phi(2.18) \approx 0.9854\).

Thus

\[ P(Y \le 80) \approx 1 - 0.9854 = 0.0146. \]

Question 6

Interarrival time between people at hospital reception: exponential with mean 30 seconds. Let \(T\) be time between arrivals; then \(T \sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda)\) where mean \(1/\lambda = 30\), so \(\lambda = 1/30\) per second.

We want: approximate probability that at most 40 people arrive in 18 minutes.

Step 1: Convert to a counting process

If interarrival times are exponential and independent, arrivals follow a Poisson process with rate \(\lambda = 1/30\) per second.

18 minutes = \(18 \times 60 = 1080\) seconds.

Mean number of arrivals in 18 minutes: \[ \lambda t = \frac{1}{30} \times 1080 = 36. \]

So number of arrivals \(N\) in 18 min is Poisson(36).

We want \(P(N \le 40)\).

Step 2: CLT approximation

Approximate \(N \sim \text{Poisson}(36)\) by \(Y \sim N(36, 36)\).

Use continuity correction:

\[ P(N \le 40) \approx P(Y \le 40.5). \]

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{40.5 - 36}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{4.5}{6} = 0.75. \]

Thus: \[ P(N \le 40) \approx \Phi(0.75). \]

\(\Phi(0.75) \approx 0.7734\).

So the approximate probability is 0.7734 as stated.
Statement is TRUE.

Question 7

At a gas station, each customer buys a random amount of gas \(X\) uniformly distributed on \([10, 40]\) liters. Daily there are 300 customers, independent.

We want the approximate probability that the daily total gas \(S_{300}\) is greater than 7410 L.

Step 1: Mean and variance of a single purchase

Uniform \([a,b]\):
\[ E(X) = \frac{a + b}{2}, \quad V(X) = \frac{(b - a)^2}{12}. \]

Here, \(a = 10\), \(b = 40\).

\[ E(X) = \frac{10 + 40}{2} = 25. \]

\[ V(X) = \frac{(40 - 10)^2}{12} = \frac{30^2}{12} = \frac{900}{12} = 75. \]

Step 2: Sum over 300 customers

Let \(S_{300} = X_1 + \cdots + X_{300}\).

\[ E(S_{300}) = 300 \cdot 25 = 7500. \]

\[ V(S_{300}) = 300 \cdot 75 = 22500. \]

\(\sigma_{S} = \sqrt{22500} = 150.\)

By CLT: \[ S_{300} \approx N(7500, 22500). \]

Step 3: Probability \(P(S_{300} > 7410)\)

Use no continuity correction since the total is continuous.

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{7410 - 7500}{150} = \frac{-90}{150} = -0.6. \]

We want \(P(S_{300} > 7410) = P(Z > -0.6)\).

\[ P(Z > -0.6) = 1 - P(Z \le -0.6) = 1 - (1 - \Phi(0.6)) = \Phi(0.6). \]

So the approximate probability is \(\Phi(0.6)\), as stated.

Statement is TRUE.

Question 8

Rice demand per day: \(X\) with mean 40 kg and standard deviation 5 kg.

Assume a year with 364 days. We order 14500 kg for the year. We want probability that this stock suffices (i.e., total demand \(S\) in the year ≤ 14500).

Step 1: Sum of daily demands

Let \(X_1, \ldots, X_{364}\) be daily independent demands (same distribution).

\[ E(X_i) = \mu = 40, \quad \sigma = 5, \quad V(X_i) = 25. \]

Total yearly demand: \[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{364} X_i. \]

\[ E(S) = 364 \cdot 40 = 14560. \]

\[ V(S) = 364 \cdot 25 = 9100. \]

\(\sigma_S = \sqrt{9100} \approx 95.394.\)

Step 2: CLT approximation

By CLT: \[ S \approx N(14560, 9100). \]

We want \[ P(S \le 14500). \]

Standardize: \[ Z = \frac{14500 - 14560}{\sqrt{9100}} = \frac{-60}{95.394} \approx -0.6289. \]

So \[ P(S \le 14500) \approx \Phi(-0.6289) = 1 - \Phi(0.6289). \]

\(\Phi(0.6289) \approx 0.7357\). Then \[ P(S \le 14500) \approx 1 - 0.7357 = 0.2643. \]

Thus probability is approximately 0.2643, as given.

Statement is TRUE.
